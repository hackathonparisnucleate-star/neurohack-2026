Two tracks, one theme

Every team picks one challenge subject and one track. The track defines your approach and what you'll be judged on — not which problem you work on.

AI / BIOINFORMATICS TRACK
Build something that runs
For researchers who want to build a working computational output over the weekend
WHAT YOU'LL DO

Teams in the AI track work toward a functional computational prototype — something that actually runs on real or synthetic data by Sunday. AWS GPU compute and shared Jupyter notebook environments are provided from day one, with a coding agent built directly into the notebook. You bring the ideas; we provide the infrastructure.

This track rewards technical ambition and execution. You don't need to solve everything — a well-scoped, well-implemented prototype that demonstrates a clear idea is more valuable than a half-finished pipeline trying to do too much.

WHAT YOU'LL BE JUDGED ON

The jury will evaluate AI track submissions on three dimensions:

  • Scientific relevance — does your approach address the challenge in a meaningful way?
  • Technical ambition — is the methodology novel or non-trivial?
  • Progress made — what did you actually build and demonstrate in 36 hours?

You can go as deep into machine learning as you want — classifiers, clustering, generative models, synthetic data pipelines. The jury includes people who will understand the technical depth.

EXAMPLE OUTPUTS
Novel biomarker pipeline ML classifier on omics data Digital phenotyping prototype Closed-loop delivery system model Synthetic dataset + validation Spatial transcriptomics analysis
BLUE SKY TRACK
Define what nobody has asked yet
For researchers who want to propose a bold, original scientific direction
WHAT YOU'LL DO

The Blue Sky track is for teams who want to think big without being constrained by what's immediately buildable. No code required. Instead, you'll develop a rigorous, original research proposal — a new therapeutic approach, a novel validation strategy, a bold hypothesis about neuro-immune interaction that hasn't been tested.

Think of it as writing the grant proposal you wish existed. You'll still need to engage seriously with the science — hand-wavy ideas won't impress this jury. But the output is a compelling argument, not a working prototype.

WHAT YOU'LL BE JUDGED ON

The jury will evaluate Blue Sky submissions on three dimensions:

  • Scientific originality — is this genuinely novel, or has it been proposed before?
  • Feasibility — is there a credible path from this idea to a real experiment or product?
  • Impact potential — if it works, how much does it matter?

The best Blue Sky submissions often combine a bold scientific claim with a concrete first experiment — showing you know how to test the idea, not just imagine it.

EXAMPLE OUTPUTS
Novel gene therapy proposal New delivery mechanism Validation study design Translational roadmap New therapeutic target hypothesis Clinical triage product concept
