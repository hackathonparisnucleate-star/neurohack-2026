Three real problems to solve

All challenges are defined by our research and industry partners. Teams pick one and work on it for the full weekend, in either the AI or Blue Sky track.

01
Servier × Paris Brain Institute
Microglia in ALS

Microglia are the brain's resident immune cells — and in ALS they shift from neuroprotective to neurotoxic states as disease progresses. This challenge asks teams to characterise that transition using public transcriptomics data and propose either a novel biomarker or a therapeutic strategy targeting microglial polarisation. Proposed by Julien Verdier's lab at the Paris Brain Institute in partnership with Servier.

Core question

Can we identify a transcriptomic or functional signature that distinguishes neuroprotective from neurotoxic microglial states in ALS — and could that signature serve as a tractable therapeutic target or early biomarker?

Guiding sub-questions
S1

Which gene modules or cell states are consistently dysregulated in ALS microglia across multiple datasets — and do they cluster around known inflammatory pathways (complement, TLR, NFκB)?

S2

Is there a minimal transcriptomic signature (gene set or cell state classifier) that separates ALS microglia from healthy controls with high specificity?

S3

What would a therapeutic intervention look like — gene therapy, small molecule, or antibody — targeting the identified microglial state? What is the translational path?

Available datasets
GSE21138 GSE17612 Human Cell Atlas — Microglia 10x Genomics — CNS OpenScience Datasets CommonMind Consortium
Key references

Dantzer R et al. From inflammation to sickness and depression. Nat Rev Neurosci. 2008.

Kelley KW et al. Cytokine-induced sickness behavior. Brain Behav Immun. 2003.

Masuda T et al. Spatial and temporal heterogeneity of mouse and human microglia at single-cell resolution. Nature. 2019.

02
Paris Brain Institute
NeuroOncology & Glioblastoma

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive primary brain tumour, with a median survival of 15 months. Its immune microenvironment — dominated by immunosuppressive microglia and tumour-associated macrophages — is a major obstacle to effective immunotherapy. This challenge asks teams to characterise the spatial and cellular heterogeneity of the GBM immune microenvironment and propose strategies to overcome immunosuppression.

Core question

Can spatial and single-cell transcriptomics reveal actionable immune microenvironment subtypes in GBM — and what therapeutic strategies (CAR-T, checkpoint, cytokine engineering) do those subtypes suggest?

Guiding sub-questions
S1

What are the dominant immune cell populations in the GBM microenvironment, and how do their spatial distributions relate to tumour core vs. infiltrating edge?

S2

Can we identify transcriptomic signatures of immunosuppressive vs. inflammatory tumour-associated macrophage (TAM) states — and what upstream regulators drive the switch?

S3

Which immune subtypes would be most amenable to existing or novel therapeutic targeting? Propose a prioritised intervention strategy with a translational rationale.

Available datasets
10x Visium — GBM spatial TCGA-GBM GEO: GSE84465 CGGA (Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas) CommonMind Ivy GAP (Allen Brain)
Key references

Neftel C et al. An integrative model of cellular states, plasticity, and genetics for human glioblastoma. Cell. 2019.

Darmanis S et al. Single-cell RNA-seq analysis of infiltrating neoplastic cells at the migrating front of human glioblastoma. Cell Rep. 2017.

Ginhoux F et al. Fate mapping analysis reveals that adult microglia derive from primitive macrophages. Science. 2010.

03
Digital Phenotyping × Mental Health
Digital Phenotyping Meets Biology

Inflammation-driven "sickness behavior" — fatigue, anhedonia, psychomotor slowing, sleep disruption, appetite change — is a well-characterised neuro-immune phenomenon. Standard mood trackers can't distinguish it from garden-variety depression. This challenge asks teams to design, and optionally prototype, a digital phenotyping product that specifically detects the cytokine-driven pattern — and sketch what a real validation study would look like.

Core question

Can a low-friction digital phenotyping product that combines passive wearable signals and an engaging active self-report layer surrogate-detect the cytokine-driven "sickness behavior" pattern specifically — rather than just detecting low mood in general? How do you validate it? And could it become an early-warning or triage product?

Three guiding sub-questions, in order
S1 · Signal design

Which combination of passive and active signals would plausibly capture the sickness-behavior cluster (fatigue, anhedonia, sleep disruption, appetite change, psychomotor slowing) as distinct from a general low-mood/depression pattern? What is the minimal signal set specific to inflammation-driven presentation rather than a generic mood tracker?

S2 · Engagement

How do you design the active self-report layer so daily adherence stays high enough for the signal to be useful over weeks, not just a one-off survey? (e.g. a "virtual pet" or avatar whose state visibly responds to the user's mood/energy self-report as a retention hook)

S3 · Validation

What would the actual study look like to confirm the digital phenotype tracks real biology? What would count as success? Consider linking flagged digital patterns to bulk RNA-seq immune-gene signatures, or using synthetic paired data calibrated to real published effect sizes (e.g. Koeneman et al., Hirten et al.).

Product concept ideas

Teams could build a consumer or clinical monitoring product with up to three layers: (1) a passive wearable layer — an app/smartwatch pairing capturing HRV, sleep, and activity; (2) an optional visual layer — a daily photo or 5-second video capturing skin and eye appearance; (3) an engaging self-report layer — daily logging with a retention hook (e.g. a "virtual pet" that responds to your reported mood/energy). No real dataset is strictly necessary — this can be a design/concept track, but teams can also use real data from participants on-site.

Available datasets
Baigutanova 2025 — HRV + PHQ-9 (n=49, 4wk) PMData (Simula) — Fitbit + wellness diary WESAD — wearable stress + affect PhysioNet — induced stress sessions DEAP — video + EEG emotion MAHNOB-HCI — multimodal affect SWELL-KW — stress + wellbeing RADAR-MDD — wearables in depression (n=623) Kaggle — smartwatch health datasets
Key references

Dantzer R, O'Connor JC, Freund GG et al. From inflammation to sickness and depression: when the immune system subjugates the brain. Nat Rev Neurosci. 2008.

Kelley KW, Bluthé RM, Dantzer R et al. Cytokine-induced sickness behavior. Brain Behav Immun. 2003.

Williams DP et al. Heart rate variability and inflammation: A meta-analysis of human studies. Brain Behav Immun. 2019.

Irwin MR, Olmstead R, Carroll JE. Sleep disturbance, sleep duration, and inflammation: A systematic review. Biol Psychiatry. 2016.

Berry N et al. Digital phenotyping for predicting relapse in psychiatric disorders. BMC Psychiatry. 2026.
