03
Psychiatry × Neuro-Immunology
Wearable Biomarkers and Inflammatory Subtypes in Psychiatric Disease: Toward Preventative Psychiatry

Inflammation-driven "sickness behavior" — fatigue, anhedonia, psychomotor slowing, sleep disruption, appetite change — is a well-characterised neuro-immune phenomenon that overlaps substantially with psychiatric symptom clusters. Standard mood trackers and clinical assessments cannot distinguish immune-driven presentations from idiopathic depression or anxiety. This challenge asks teams to design, and optionally prototype, a digital phenotyping product that specifically detects inflammation-linked psychiatric signatures using wearable and self-report signals — and to sketch what a rigorous validation study would look like.

Core question

Can a low-friction digital phenotyping product that combines passive wearable signals and an engaging active self-report layer surrogate-detect the cytokine-driven "sickness behavior" pattern specifically — rather than just detecting low mood in general? How do you validate it against real immune biology? And could it become an early-warning or triage product toward preventative psychiatry?

Three guiding sub-questions, in order
S1 · Signal design

Which combination of passive and active signals would plausibly capture the sickness-behavior cluster (fatigue, anhedonia, sleep disruption, appetite change, psychomotor slowing) as distinct from a general low-mood or depression pattern? What is the minimal signal set specific to inflammation-driven psychiatric presentation rather than a generic mood tracker?

S2 · Engagement

How do you design the active self-report layer so daily adherence stays high enough for the signal to be useful over weeks — not just a one-off survey? Consider gamification, contextual prompting, or social hooks (e.g. a "virtual pet" or avatar whose visible state responds to the user's mood and energy self-report as a retention mechanism).

S3 · Validation

What would the actual study look like to confirm the digital phenotype tracks real immune biology? What would count as success? Consider linking flagged digital patterns to immune-gene expression signatures, or constructing synthetic paired datasets calibrated to real published effect sizes (e.g. Koeneman et al. on HRV preceding cytokine rises, Hirten et al. on HRV predicting inflammatory flares alongside CRP).

Product concept & approach ideas

Teams could build a consumer or clinical monitoring product with up to three layers: (1) a passive wearable layer — an app/smartwatch pairing capturing HRV, sleep, and activity as core physiological signals; (2) an optional visual layer — a daily photo or short video capturing skin and eye appearance as proxies for inflammatory state; (3) an engaging self-report layer — daily logging with a retention hook (e.g. a virtual pet or avatar whose visible state responds to reported mood and energy). No real dataset is strictly necessary — this can be a design and concept track. Teams can also use real data collected from participants on-site, or go deep into machine learning: training classifiers on wearable time-series to distinguish inflammatory from non-inflammatory symptom patterns, building unsupervised clustering pipelines on multi-modal data, or developing synthetic data generation strategies grounded in published immune-HRV correlations.
